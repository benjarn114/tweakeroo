# 1. What it is
Tweakeroo is a free application made by one person, with no company behind it. It changes Windows and Fortnite settings, opens the game at the resolution you choose, shows your FPS and gives advice about your PC.

# 3. What it changes on your PC
It only changes what you turn on. It needs administrator permission because it changes the registry, services, scheduled tasks, the power plan, the network card, the NVIDIA profile and Fortnite's settings. Before the first change it saves a copy called Original and lets you go back to it. It also tries to create a Windows restore point, but on some computers Windows doesn't allow it. If you create a custom resolution, it is stored in your graphics driver.

# 4. Risks
Changing system settings can cause failures, loss of features or different results depending on the computer. Settings marked Risk can lower Windows security. Back up what matters to you and create a restore point before you start. You decide what to turn on, and you do so at your own responsibility.

# 7.1. Macros
The Macros section is optional and off by default. It is meant for practice in Creative and sends nothing in other modes. It sends keyboard and mouse presses, which Epic forbids in tournaments and which can lead to penalties or losing the account, also in Creative. It doesn't hide its use from the anti-cheat. If you turn it on, you accept that risk.

# Privacy
Tweakeroo has no accounts, ads or analytics, and doesn't send your data to its author.

# What it reads on your PC
Your parts, the settings it can change, Fortnite's settings and the logs the game leaves. From the logs it only keeps the date, mode, length and FPS of each match. If you press Read my Fortnite keys or choose a macro's key, it reads the keys from your local copy of the game's settings and doesn't send them. If you turn on a macro, it checks whether the keys you chose are pressed and reads the game's log to know whether you are in Creative; it doesn't store what you press. To show what uses the most memory, it looks at the names of open programs and doesn't store them.

# Where it keeps it
On your PC, in your user's AppData\Local\Tweakeroo folder. Nobody else receives it. You can delete it when uninstalling or by removing that folder.

# Internet connections
It connects to GitHub to look for and download updates. If you press Test connection, it pings your router, 1.1.1.1 (Cloudflare) and Epic's measuring servers; the results stay on your PC. Like any website, GitHub receives your IP address and handles it under its privacy policy. Opening Fortnite uses Epic's launcher, which has its own terms.
